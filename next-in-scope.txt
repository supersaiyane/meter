NEXT IN SCOPE: what the market research of 2026-10-07 found, and what to add next
==================================================================================

Written 2026-10-07, after P8 merged into main (31b14d7). A record of findings and
proposals for the owner to decide on. Nothing here is approved or planned yet.
Market figures are as reported by their source, not measured by Meter. Meter
figures come from docs/registry.yaml and the plans on 2026-10-07.


1. THE BOTTOM LINE
------------------

Meter's strongest claim, that every number says how far it can be trusted, is one
no competitor makes structurally. It cannot yet prove that claim, because no adapter
has ever called a real AWS account. During 2026 the market made several of the
things Meter is still building free or common. Meter wins by becoming the FinOps
platform whose numbers are proven against the invoice, then by owning AI cost,
owners and savings credit, and pull request based action. More breadth before real
data is the wrong next move.


2. THE MARKET IN 2026
---------------------

AWS (free, native)
  - AWS FinOps Agent, public preview 2026-06-09, us-east-1, no charge in preview.
    Natural language cost questions, anomaly investigation correlated with
    CloudTrail ("what changed and who changed it"), Slack and Jira, recurring
    reports. Read only.
  - Billing and Cost Management MCP server, 2025-08.
  - At FinOps X 2026: Savings Plans coverage targets, automatic cost and forecast
    explanations, twice as many idle recommendations, Bedrock cost by app, agent
    and user.
  - Published a four tier automation model: read only, human approved mutation,
    rule based automation, autonomy with boundaries.

IBM (Turbonomic, Cloudability, Kubecost, Apptio)
  - A Leader in the 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Cloud Financial Management
    Tools, published 2025-09-15, 13 vendors evaluated.
  - Turbonomic: real time sizing up and down against application targets, GPU
    optimisation (8.12, NVIDIA, MIG aware), Terraform pull requests through
    GitOps (commitMode request or direct).
  - Cloudability: Conversational Insights, an MCP server, a FOCUS AI agent.
  - The suite is converging; one 2026 guide reports cross module integration
    still in progress.
  - Reviewers' complaints about Turbonomic: complex setup, a dated and dense UI,
    expensive tiered licensing, actions too aggressive without policy tuning.

Flexera
  - A Leader in the same Gartner report.
  - Bought Spot from NetApp (closed 2025-03-03), then ProsperOps and Chaos Genius
    (2026-01-06). Now autonomous commitment management across AWS, Azure and GCP,
    plus Snowflake and Databricks optimisation.
  - ProsperOps reports customers at 60.8 and 62.4 percent effective savings rate
    (vendor reported).

Others
  - Vantage: MCP server, a FinOps Agent that removes waste by policy, Autopilot
    buying Savings Plans, virtual tags.
  - CloudZero and Finout: AI cost per customer and per feature, virtual tagging.
  - CAST AI, ScaleOps: autonomous Kubernetes and GPU optimisation. Kubecost shows
    and allocates; it does not act.
  - Infracost: cost of a Terraform change in the pull request, policies, AutoFix.
  - Oracle, Azure: MCP servers for cost.
  - Agentic FinOps became a category in 2026 (PointFive, nOps Clara, cloudgov.ai
    and others).

FinOps Foundation and standards
  - FOCUS 1.3 ratified 2025-12-05. FOCUS 1.4 ratified 2026-06-04: Invoice Detail and
    Billing Period datasets, invoice reconciliation, Contract Commitment grown to
    30 columns, commitment eligibility, token fields (input, output, cached).
  - FOCUS 1.5 due 2026-12: native token and model identity, a Price Sheet dataset.
  - A FOCUS Certified Conformant program; AWS and Nebius certified first.
  - The Tokenomics Foundation (Linux Foundation, announced 2026-06-03). FinOps X
    becomes part of Tokenomicon, 2027-06-07 to 2027-06-10.

State of FinOps 2026 (1,192 respondents, over 83 billion dollars of annual cloud
spend, as reported)
  - In scope: AI 98 percent, SaaS 90, licensing 64, private cloud 57, data
    centre 48, labour 28.
  - Top priority now: workload optimisation and waste. Next twelve months:
    FinOps for AI, then AI for FinOps, then more technology categories.
  - Most wanted tool features: granular AI spend (tokens, LLM requests, GPU use),
    costing before deployment, one view across all technology spend.
  - The unsolved problem: "Once you fix it, it's gone." Developers get no lasting
    credit for savings they make.


3. WHERE METER ALREADY LEADS
----------------------------

  - Provenance on every figure: measured, modelled or forecast, two timestamps,
    typed failures, never a zero for a failure.
  - The MCP server goes through the same API and scope checks as a person, so an
    agent can never see more than its human.
  - Query cost governance for observability (P2) and quotas (P5): little
    competition in depth.
  - The platform meters itself, on the front page.
  - FOCUS native from the first day, so another provider is a connector.


4. WHAT COULD HAVE BEEN BETTER
------------------------------

  1. Breadth before proof. Nine sub projects and 42 features delivered, and no
     adapter has made a real call (ISS-002). CUR 2.0 has never been enabled
     (ISS-003). No Terraform stack has been applied (ISS-122). A deployed API would
     refuse to start (ISS-250).
  2. The interface was polished before the foundation was signed off. P0 is code
     complete and not signed off (ISS-111, ISS-122). FEAT-004, FEAT-005 and FEAT-007
     are still In build in the registry.
  3. The only CRITICAL issue is the product's central gap: ISS-020, "It reports,
     it does not prevent".
  4. No owner attribution (ISS-021), so a finding, and later a pull request, has
     nobody to go to.
  5. FOCUS 1.1 is pinned while the standard reached 1.4. ISS-022, the cost model
     never back tested against the bill, is what 1.4's invoice reconciliation is for.
  6. Owner decisions block the action loop: OPEN-01 identity provider, OPEN-02
     payers, OPEN-05 ticketing, OPEN-11 delivery, OPEN-13 code host.
  7. FEAT-039 (anomaly narration from CloudTrail) and the P7 assistant now overlap
     the free AWS FinOps Agent.
  8. The gate is slow for small changes: about 15.5 minutes of backend tests for a
     13 line documentation change on 2026-10-07 (measured, 927 and 935 seconds).
  9. Possible registry drift: FEAT-023 (anomalies) and FEAT-024 (commitments) say
     Specified while P1 says both were built. To reconcile.


5. WHAT TO ADD, IN PRIORITY ORDER (proposals for the owner)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Do not build where the market is now free: a generic chat box over cost data and
CloudTrail anomaly explanation. Take in AWS's explanations and add what AWS
cannot: scope, owners, provenance and other providers.

  1. Proven numbers. Connect one real payer, run one billing period, reconcile to
     the invoice with FOCUS 1.4, and show the match rate on the front page.
     Closes ISS-002, ISS-003, ISS-122, ISS-250 and ISS-022.
  2. FinOps for AI. Bedrock by app, agent and user; OpenAI and Anthropic API cost;
     GPU use; cost per token, request and feature; FOCUS 1.4 and 1.5 token fields.
  3. Owners and a savings ledger. Owners from tags, the service catalogue and
     CloudTrail creators; every saving credited to the engineer whose change made
     it, verified against the bill. Answers ISS-021 and the survey's unsolved
     problem.
  4. Graded autonomy through pull requests. Finish FEAT-040 once OPEN-13 is
     answered; auto merge low risk classes (gp2 to gp3 from P9, unattached
     volumes, old snapshots) under policy, with measured before and after.
  5. Commitments. Coverage and eligibility (FOCUS 1.4), effective savings rate as
     the headline, purchase plans behind approval. Partner rather than build
     autonomous buying.
  6. Costing before deployment. Price a Terraform plan in the pull request from
     P9's catalogue, then compare the estimate with the bill a month later.
  7. Performance aware rightsizing (FEAT-058), down and up against service level
     targets, for EC2, RDS and EKS only.
  8. More providers through FOCUS: Azure, GCP, OCI, Snowflake, Databricks and
     Grafana Cloud, which now exports FOCUS and helps P2.
  9. Carbon beside cost, using AWS's carbon tool, which now covers Scope 3, for
     EU buyers under CSRD.

Against Turbonomic: minutes to first value, the P8 interface, plain pricing,
every action a reviewed pull request with provenance, one data model instead of
three acquired products. Do not chase on premises VMware or its analytics engine.

Suggested order: real data and reconciliation, then AI cost and owners, then
pull request autonomy and commitments, then costing before deployment and more
providers. After P9. Shape P9's price catalogue to FOCUS 1.5's Price Sheet.

One task at a time, weekdays only, no holidays, starting 2026-10-08. Everything
can be built against fakes as the earlier phases were; nothing is proven live
